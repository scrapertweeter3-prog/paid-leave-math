Nobody prorates December

A fully loaded senior engineer at $18,290 a month costs $105.52 an hour, $844.16 for a working day. The rate quietly assumes the desk is occupied. The salaried calendar says otherwise: a year holds 260 weekdays and 32 of them are paid days off, 15 PTO, 11 federal holidays, 6 sick, leaving 228 days on which the roadmap actually moves. The company pays all 260 at full rate, and 87.7 percent of them produce work.

Price the dark days. Thirty two days at $844.16 is $27,013.12 a year of salary spent while nothing ships, 4.5 months of an unlimited development subscription bought and never received. Nothing prorates them. The offer letter pays the same in July as it does in the dead week between Christmas and New Year, and the missing days never appear as a line anywhere.

Flat agency retainers inherit the same calendar and round it up. December carries 23 weekdays and the holiday stretch typically darkens ten of them; a $12,000 retainer does not pro-rate the month, so $5,217.39 of it, 43.5 percent, funds days nobody works. That is 87 percent of an entire subscription month spent on an empty bench, every year, before a single change order.

The subscription ledger has no paid time off line because it has no calendar to pay for. A request card filed on December 24 prices the same as one filed on January 2, the queue works every working day of the year, and the buyer never funds a day the desk was dark. Before comparing a hire or a retainer to a subscription, count the days each option pays for and subtract the days the work can actually happen. The difference is the paid time off line hiding inside every salary.
